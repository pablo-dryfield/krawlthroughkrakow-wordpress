## Legal basis used for the review

Polish Civil Code Articles 385¹–385³ address unfair consumer terms, including liability exclusions and unilateral changes. Articles 471–474 concern contractual responsibility. Article 921 requires a period for competing for a best-work/activity prize and addresses judging and rights in the winning work. These provisions support the flagged risks; application to KTK's exact facts remains an assessment, not a court ruling. [Civil Code, 2026 consolidated text](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2026/795/text/I/D20260795.pdf).

Polish electronic-communications law Article 398 requires prior permission for the covered commercial/direct-marketing communications. An ordinary website visit or request for help should not be drafted as automatic marketing enrolment. No marketing permission is created by the owner's approval of copy. [Electronic Communications Law, current codified text](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2024/1221/text/U/D20241221Lj.pdf).

The Commission explains that personal-data retention must reflect necessity and relevant obligations, with erasure or review limits. A general wish to keep enquiries indefinitely supplies neither a continuing purpose nor an exception. GDPR Article 13(2)(a) allows retention criteria when a period cannot be specified; that does not authorise unlimited storage. [Commission: storage limitation](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/principles-gdpr_en), [GDPR](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679).

The notice needs the controller, purposes, lawful bases, retention, recipients/transfers, rights and complaint information. The EDPB distinguishes necessary pre-contractual processing from separate marketing purposes and explains that legitimate interest needs an actual assessment. Recruitment must not be assigned a legal basis or fixed expiry solely by analogy with a booking enquiry. [Commission: information and rights](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-individuals_en), [EDPB: lawful processing](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sme/be-compliant/process-personal-data-lawfully_en).

The Consumer Rights Act addresses pre-contract information and contract confirmation; Article 38(1)(12) concerns specified-date accommodation/leisure and related services. Eligibility for that exception does not settle every cancellation, non-performance or refund question. [Consumer Rights Act, 2026 consolidated text](https://eli.gov.pl/api/acts/DU/2026/1244/text/I/D20261244.pdf), [UOKiK: withdrawal exceptions](https://prawakonsumenta.uokik.gov.pl/prawo-odstapienia-od-umowy/wylaczenia-prawa-do-odstapienia/). Applicable-law questions also require attention to Rome I's scope and exceptions; consumer protections are preserved in the proposed clause without asserting that every customer receives the same home-law result. [Rome I](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2008/593).
