Exploring Krakow’s Historic Sites

Discover the rich history of Krakow by visiting its numerous historic sites, including Wawel Hill, now an art museum, and the biggest medieval market square in Europe.

Wawel Hill Art Museum

The majestic Wawel Castle houses a remarkable collection of artwork, showcasing Poland’s cultural heritage, including Leonardo da Vinci’s famous painting “Lady with an Ermine.”

Medieval Market Square

Krakow’s Main Market Square is one of Europe’s largest medieval squares, featuring historical landmarks such as the Cloth Hall (Sukiennice) and Town Hall Tower. It is one of the perfect Krakow budget activities.

Stunning Churches

  • St. Mary’s Basilica: A Gothic masterpiece with a wooden altarpiece carved by Veit Stoss and hourly trumpet call played from its taller tower.
  • St. Joseph’s Church: An impressive Neo-Gothic church featuring beautiful stained glass windows and intricate details.
  • Corpus Christi Basilica: A stunning example of Polish Gothic architecture with a richly decorated interior and an impressive Baroque high altar.

Experience the beauty and history of Krakow budget activities such as churches and landmarks for yourself.

Enjoying Nature in Krakow

Don’t miss out on Krakow’s natural beauty – immerse yourself in picturesque spots for nature lovers and outdoor enthusiasts alike.

Planty Park Gardens

Stay within the city limits and head over to Planty Park, a 42-hectare green space encircling Old Town with 30 smaller gardens filled with graves, tombs, and mausoleums designed by various artists.

Enjoy fresh air and beautiful landscapes at countless other parks scattered throughout Krakow – one of the free things you can look forward too.

Uncovering History at Museums

Dive deeper into Krakow’s fascinating past by exploring museums like the Rynek Underground Museum, which takes visitors on a journey through time as they explore the foundations of Old Town.

Immerse yourself in interactive exhibits and displays that tell stories about Jewish life in historical times at the Rynek Underground Museum, located beneath the Main Market Square.

  • Archaeological Discoveries: The Rynek Underground Museum showcases fascinating discoveries related to Krakow’s ancient history, making it a must-visit for history buffs.

Gain a better understanding of Krakow’s storied past by exploring other historical sites around town or attending free lectures offered by local universities or cultural institutions.

Participating in Cultural Events

Experience local culture firsthand by attending free or low-cost cultural events throughout town, from outdoor concerts to street festivals celebrating traditional Polish customs and holidays.

Outdoor Concerts

Enjoy Krakow’s vibrant music scene at an outdoor concert in a park, square, or public space during the summer months, and check out Karnet Krakow for upcoming events.

Street Festivals

  • Juwenalia: Join locals at this annual student festival in May for live music, sports competitions, parades, and more.
  • Pierogi Festival: Celebrate Poland’s famous dumplings every August at Main Market Square with affordable food stalls offering various types of pierogi.
  • Krakow Christmas Market: Get into the festive spirit during the winter season at Krakow’s Christmas market, where you can shop for unique gifts and try traditional Polish treats like oscypek cheese.

Experience the local atmosphere without spending too much by participating in Krakow’s numerous cultural activities.

Eats Around Town

When in Krakow, you must partake of the local fare and fortunately, it won’t break the bank to savor some scrumptious regional food.

Local Food Markets

For fresh produce, meats, cheeses, and traditional Polish dishes like pierogi and kielbasa, head to Stary Kleparz, one of the oldest markets in town.

Budget-Friendly Restaurants

  • Milk Bars: These government-subsidized eateries offer hearty meals at incredibly low prices.
  • Zapiekanki: Grab a quick and tasty zapiekanka – an open-faced sandwich topped with mushrooms and cheese – from one of the many street vendors in Kazimierz District.
  • Pizza Parlors: Satisfy your pizza cravings without breaking the bank at spots like Pizza Hut Szewska Street.

No matter your dietary needs or preferences, Krakow has something to offer for those on a budget.

Free Walking Tours

Discover the city’s rich history and hidden gems with free walking tours led by knowledgeable guides, perfect for budget-conscious travelers.

Historical Walking Tours

Dive into Krakow’s past with a historical walking tour that covers significant landmarks such as Wawel Castle, St. Mary’s Basilica, and the Cloth Hall, led by experienced guides who are passionate about sharing their knowledge of Krakow’s intriguing history.

Themed Walking Tours

  • Jewish Quarter Tour: Explore Kazimierz District, once a thriving Jewish community, and visit synagogues, cemeteries, and other important sites related to Jewish culture in Krakow. Learn more about its role during World War II at the Galicia Jewish Museum.
  • Ghosts & Legends Tour: Unveil chilling tales of supernatural events throughout centuries-old streets on this unique tour that’s both spooky and entertaining.
  • Socialist Architecture Tour: Discover remnants of Poland’s communist era through iconic buildings like Nowa Huta district or Central Railway Station

Embark on any of these free walking tours in Krakow for valuable insights into this captivating city without putting a dent in your wallet.

Exploring Krakow’s Vibrant Street Art Scene

Contemporary art aficionados should not miss Krakow’s vibrant street art scene, which features amazing works from artists around the world that exhibit creativity and celebrate cultural heritage. Getting to see these masterpieces is just one of the free things that will have you coming back to the city for more.

Murals in Kazimierz District

Discover striking murals in the historic Jewish district of Kazimierz, such as “Judah” by Pil Peled or “Bawol Square” by Broken Fingaz Crew, that reflect on the area’s rich history and cultural heritage.

FAQs in Relation to Krakow Budget Activities

How to Save Money in Krakow?

Save money in Krakow by staying at budget accommodations, eating at local food markets and inexpensive restaurants, using public transportation or walking instead of taxis, participating in free walking tours, exploring the city’s street art scene, and taking advantage of free museum days and discounted entrance fees for attractions.

How Many Days to Spend in Krakow?

Explore Krakow’s main attractions such as Wawel Hill Art Museum, Medieval Market Square, Planty Park Gardens, and Kazimierz District in a minimum of three days, or consider spending five to seven days for a more immersive experience.

How Much Does it Cost per Day in Krakow?

The average daily cost for a tourist visiting Krakow is around $40-$60 USD per person, including accommodation (budget options), meals at affordable eateries or food markets, public transportation costs, and sightseeing expenses with some free activities included.
