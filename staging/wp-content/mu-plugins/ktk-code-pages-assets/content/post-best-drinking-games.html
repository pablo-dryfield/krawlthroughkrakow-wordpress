KTK favourites for the table

Start with a game everyone can follow, explain the house rules and keep the first round simple. A tower of blocks, a deck of cards or a few good questions can give a new group plenty to talk about.

Choose a game that fits your table and the time you have. Short rounds make it easy to switch between games and keep the evening moving.

KTK Drunken Jenga

A tower of blocks, a group of friends and KTK's party prompts: Drunken Jenga is a fun activity to bring to the table before a night out. Take turns pulling a block, reading its prompt and placing it on top. Every move changes the tower, so even a confident player can find the next turn a little more interesting.

Download the KTK Drunken Jenga rules (PDF, 47.8 MB) for our game prompts. Set up your blocks, get the group around the table and see who can keep the tower standing.

Quick team challenges

Try a drawing round, a team quiz or naming a song from a clue. Set a short timer, keep score and swap teams between rounds to get different people working together.

Team quiz showdown

Split into small teams and choose a few categories: travel, music, films or unusual general knowledge. Give each team the same time to answer and award a point for a correct response. Let the team with the best score choose the next category.

Never have I ever

One person begins with "Never have I ever..." and the answers reveal who has a story worth hearing. Travel mishaps, hobbies and surprising talents give you plenty of material for the next round.

Find ideas in our KTK Never Have I Ever prompts (PDF). Keep a few questions ready, then let the conversation take over when an answer deserves a story.

Tabletop target game

Arrange target cups at each end of a suitable table and split into two teams. Take turns throwing a ball towards the other team's cups and score a point for each successful shot.

For a straightforward skill game, use empty cups and agree the number of turns before starting. Check that the table is available for the game and leave enough room around it for the players.

Card games for a night with friends

A deck of cards is easy to carry and gives you several games from the same small piece of kit. Pick your house rules, decide how ties work and deal the first round.

Try a quick guessing game to warm up, then move to bluffing or matching when everyone has settled into the rhythm.

King's Cup

Draw a card in turn and use its rank to choose the next rule or prompt. Categories, rhymes and a change in turn direction keep the table alert as the game develops.

Use the KTK King's Cup rules (PDF) for our version, and keep the rule list handy for the next card.

Higher or lower

Reveal a card and ask the next player whether the following card will rank higher or lower. Decide how an equal rank works, then see how many correct guesses you can make in a row.

For a group round, move around the table after each guess and keep a running score. A streak makes every new card a little more suspenseful.

Pyramid

Lay cards face down in a small pyramid and deal the rest to the players. Reveal one pyramid card at a time; players with the same rank can put down a matching card for a point.

Decide how many matches count before you start. Working from the bottom row to the top gives the round a clear finish and leaves room for a rematch.

Bluffing cards

Take turns placing cards face down and announcing their rank, following the sequence your group has agreed. Other players can challenge the claim and turn the cards over.

If the player was bluffing, they take the pile; if the claim was true, the challenger takes it. The aim is to get rid of your cards, and a convincing poker face helps.

Electricity

Reveal cards around the table and look for a connection, such as the same rank or an adjacent number. Count the connected cards until someone breaks the chain.

Give a point for each connection and keep track of your longest run. Decide how aces connect before the first round.

Little or no equipment

No cards or blocks to hand? Try a naming round, a memory challenge or a few good questions. These games can start with almost no setup, making them useful while your group is getting ready for the evening.

Five Second Rule

Choose a category and challenge the next player to name three examples in five seconds. Three cities, three breakfast foods or three film titles sound easy until the timer starts.

Count correct answers and move to the next player. Keep the categories coming and see which question produces the most unexpected response.

Most likely to

Read a prompt such as "most likely to plan a spontaneous trip" or "most likely to remember every song lyric". Count down together and point to the person who fits the description.

The fun is in hearing why the group picked someone. Mix familiar habits with travel and music questions to keep the round lively.

Cardboard prompt board

Draw large spaces on a clean sheet of cardboard and fill them with prompts: name a film, draw a landmark or choose the next category. Use a counter to select a space and work your way around the board.

Heads or tails

Pick a side, toss a coin and count your correct guesses over a set number of turns. Play head-to-head or let the whole table guess the same toss.

Keep a tally of the longest streak and see who can beat it. It is a quick game for the moments between longer rounds.

More group games

Mix up the evening with a different kind of game. A skill challenge, a counting round and a music quiz give different people their chance to shine.

Flip Cup

Line up empty reusable cups on a stable table and split into teams. The first player flips their cup from the edge, trying to land it upside down, before the next teammate takes a turn.

Count successful landings or set a shared team target. A little cheering can make even a small table game feel like a tournament.

Buzz

Count around the group, replacing a chosen set of numbers with "buzz". Multiples of seven make a good starting point, then you can add another rule once everyone has the idea.

Start a new round after a mistake and see how far the group gets. The longer the sequence, the more concentration it takes.

Music guessing round

Give a song clue or play a short excerpt where music is permitted, then challenge the group to name the artist or track. Try a theme such as film soundtracks, holiday favourites or songs from one decade.

Play as teams and keep a score, or let the person who guesses correctly choose the next song.

Night-out bingo

Make a bingo grid of light moments from an evening out: hearing a favourite song, learning a new phrase or discovering a new food. Mark off the squares as the evening unfolds and compare your cards later.

Mix common moments with a few that are harder to find, then see who gets the first full row.

Games and a night in Krakow

For games away from your own space, check the venue's arrangements and keep the city's Respect Krakow visitor guidance in mind when planning where to meet.

Start with a toast

A toast can mark the start of the evening or a reunion with friends. Give each person a turn to name a highlight from the trip, then choose the first game for the table.

For a different round, have everyone add one word to a short group toast and see what the last person has to remember.

Team target challenge

For a throwing-game variation, use a suitable permitted space and a simple points target. Split into teams, agree the throwing line and give each player the same number of attempts.

Count points over several rounds, then move the target or change the distance for a rematch. Choose a space with room for the ball and the players.

Truth or dare

Take turns choosing a question or a light challenge. A funny travel story, a drawing task or an improvised performance gives the group something to react to.

Use a few prepared ideas to get started, then build your next round around the people and stories at the table.

Challenge ideas

  • Draw a Krakow landmark for teammates to guess.
  • Name a film using three clues.
  • Hum the opening of a song.
  • Invent a short fictional travel story.
  • Give a one-minute speech about a completely ordinary object.

Conversation ideas

  • What is the most unexpected thing that happened on a trip?
  • Which place would you return to tomorrow?
  • What song reminds you of a good holiday?
  • What hobby would you like to learn?
  • Which food would you introduce to the rest of the group?
