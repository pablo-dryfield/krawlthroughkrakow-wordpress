Exploring Wawel Hill

Discover the beauty of Wawel Hill, a historical and cultural gem in Krakow. Take a stroll around the lake and its picturesque trails, admiring the courtyard gardens and art museum as you immerse yourself in Wawel Hill’s rich history.

Visit Krakow’s Castle and Cathedral for free or at reduced prices

This magnificent Castle is one of Poland’s most important landmarks, showcasing stunning architecture from various periods. The castle grounds are open to visitors for free, while some parts, like the State Rooms, require an entrance fee. However, if you plan your visit for Mondays between April and October or Sundays during November and March (except holidays), admission fees are waived.

Stroll through picturesque courtyard gardens

Apart from its architectural wonders, Wawel Hill also boasts beautiful courtyard gardens where you can relax amidst lush greenery after exploring nearby attractions. These serene spaces offer spectacular views over Krakow’s Old Town skyline—perfect spots for taking memorable photos.

Walk along scenic lakeside paths

  • Lake Walk: Enjoy a leisurely stroll around Lake Drweckie situated within Wawel Hill’s grounds. The picturesque path offers stunning views of the castle and cathedral, as well as opportunities to spot local wildlife.
  • Dragon’s Den: For a more adventurous walk, head towards the legendary Dragon’s Den, a limestone cave situated beneath the Castle. According to Polish folklore, this was once home to a fearsome dragon that terrorized Krakow until it was defeated by a brave shoemaker named Skuba.

Wawel Hill is a great place to explore and appreciate the history of Krakow. From here, delve into more of the city’s past at the Rynek Underground Museum, where interactive exhibits will take you back in time to medieval Krakow.

Key Takeaway: 

Explore the historical and cultural gem of Wawel Hill in Krakow, which includes an art museum, courtyard gardens, and a lake with fantastic walking trails. Visit the magnificent Wawel Castle and Cathedral for free or at reduced prices on specific days. Stroll through picturesque courtyard gardens and walk along scenic lakeside paths to spot local wildlife or visit the legendary Dragon’s Den.

Unearthing History at Rynek Underground Museum

Delve into Krakow’s past by visiting the Rynek Underground Museum. Here you can explore the foundations of buildings that once stood in Old Town centuries ago, offering insight into what life was like during medieval times. This unique museum allows visitors to walk through history and discover fascinating stories about this beautiful city.

Explore archaeological excavations beneath Main Market Square

The Rynek Underground Museum is located directly beneath the famous Main Market Square, giving you a chance to see what lies hidden underneath this bustling area. As you wander through the underground passages, you’ll come across well-preserved remains of ancient structures such as merchant stalls and water systems. These archaeological findings provide valuable information on how people lived and worked in medieval Krakow.

Learn about daily life in medieval Krakow through interactive exhibits

This state-of-the-art museum features various interactive exhibits designed to engage visitors while teaching them about everyday life during different periods of Krakow’s history. You’ll be able to view detailed reconstructions of historical scenes, watch multimedia presentations, and even participate in hands-on activities that bring these ancient times back to life.

  • Ticket Prices: The entrance fee for adults is 12-28 PLN (approximately $2 -$6). Children under seven years old enter for free.
  • Museum Hours: The Rynek Underground Museum is open from Monday to Sunday between 10:00 a.m. and 8:00 p.m. (closed on the second Monday of the month).

Krakow Free Activities at Historic Churches

Krakow free activities include some impressive historic churches that are worth exploring without spending much money on entrance fees. These sacred spaces showcase intricate architecture and artwork, offering a glimpse into the city’s rich religious history. Some of the must-visit churches include St. Mary’s Church, St. Joseph’s Church, St. Andrew’s Church, and Corpus Christi Basilica.

Admire the intricate architecture and Artwork Inside These Sacred Spaces

Marvel at the intricate frescoes, stained glass windows, altarpieces, and sculptures of these ancient churches, crafted by renowned artists in times gone by. For instance, St. Mary’s Church is home to an exquisite wooden altarpiece crafted by Veit Stoss, a masterpiece considered one of the finest examples of Gothic art in Europe.

Attend Mass or Listen to Organ Concerts for Free

Beyond admiring their beauty, visitors can also attend mass or listen to organ concerts held within these magnificent structures for free, providing an opportunity to experience Krakow’s spiritual side while enjoying its cultural offerings simultaneously.

  • St. Mary’s Church: Masses are held daily, and you can catch the famous Hejnal Mariacki (trumpet call) every hour from the church tower. Additionally, free organ concerts take place on Sundays at noon.
  • St. Joseph’s Church: Attend mass in this beautiful neo-Gothic structure with its stunning stained glass windows and impressive frescoes.
  • St. Andrew’s Church: This Romanesque gem offers a serene atmosphere for prayer or attending mass, surrounded by ancient stone walls dating back to the 11th century.
  • Corpus Christi Basilica: Known for its Polish Baroque Architecture, visitors can attend mass or simply marvel at the magnificent artwork adorning this historic basilica.

In addition to the churches mentioned above, Krakow is home to numerous other sacred spaces that are worth exploring during your visit, each offering unique architectural styles and fascinating stories waiting to be discovered.

Marveling at historic churches is a wonderful way to appreciate Krakow’s history and culture, so why not take some time to relax in Planty Park afterwards? Enjoy the peaceful atmosphere while admiring statues and monuments dedicated to Polish heroes.

Relaxing in Planty Park

Surround yourself with lush greenery by taking a stroll around Planty Park—30 smaller gardens spanning over 42 hectares filled with graves, tombs, and mausoleums designed by various artists, making it perfect for those looking for tranquility amidst city hustle and bustle. This picturesque park encircles the entire Old Town of Krakow and offers an ideal escape from the busy streets.

Enjoy picnics under shady trees or beside beautiful fountains

The serene atmosphere of Planty Park makes it an excellent spot for a leisurely picnic. Find a comfortable spot under one of the many shady trees or near one of the enchanting fountains scattered throughout the park. Pack your own lunch or grab some delicious local snacks from nearby vendors before settling down on a bench or grassy area to enjoy your meal al fresco.

Besides being an oasis in the heart of Krakow, Planty Park serves as a gateway between different parts of this historic city. So whether you’re exploring Wawel Hill, marveling at historic churches like St. Mary’s Church, or simply enjoying Europe’s largest medieval market square, Main Market Square, make sure not to miss out on experiencing this verdant haven during your visit to Krakow.

Relaxing in Planty Park is an ideal way to spend the day and experience Krakow’s beauty. For a more immersive cultural experience, exploring Europe’s largest medieval market square is highly recommended.

Europe’s Largest Medieval Market Square

Immerse yourself in European history by visiting Krakow’s Main Market Square, the largest medieval market square in Europe, where you can admire historic buildings and watch street performers without spending a dime. This bustling area is filled with locals and tourists alike, all enjoying the vibrant atmosphere that makes this square so unique.

Explore Cloth Hall (Sukiennice) for traditional Polish souvenirs

The centerpiece of the Main Market Square, this building is an architectural gem that dates back to the 14th century. Originally serving as a hub for international trade, today it houses numerous stalls selling traditional Polish souvenirs such as amber jewelry, hand-painted pottery, and wooden crafts. Be sure to take your time browsing through these unique items while soaking up the historical ambiance of this beautiful building.

Witness hourly trumpet call from St. Mary’s Church tower

A visit to Main Market Square would not be complete without experiencing one of its most famous traditions: the hourly trumpet call (Hejnal Mariacki) from St. Mary’s Church tower. As each hour strikes, a trumpeter plays a short melody that suddenly stops mid-tune, commemorating an ancient legend about a trumpeter who was shot by an arrow while warning citizens about approaching enemies during the 13th century. This unique spectacle can be enjoyed from anywhere in the square, so make sure to keep an ear out for it during your visit.

Krakow’s Main Market Square is an absolute must for those journeying through this picturesque city, boasting a vibrant ambience and fascinating past. Whether you’re shopping for souvenirs or simply enjoying the hourly trumpet call, there’s no shortage of free activities to keep you entertained in Europe’s largest medieval market square.

Key Takeaway: Krakow’s Main Market Square is the largest medieval market square in Europe and offers a vibrant atmosphere filled with street performers, historic buildings, and traditional Polish souvenirs.
