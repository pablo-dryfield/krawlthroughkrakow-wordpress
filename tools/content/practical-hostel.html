A shared room works better when guests can enjoy their trip without taking over somebody else's sleep, space or privacy. These seven hostel etiquette tips are practical things you can do before heading out, when using shared facilities and when returning from Krakow nightlife.

Start with your hostel's own information. Reception hours, entry arrangements, room occupancy, storage and quiet periods vary. Read the booking details and ask staff about anything unclear instead of relying on a rule from a different hostel.

1. Respect quiet periods

A party hostel is still somewhere people have paid to sleep. Ask about its quiet periods and which social areas remain available late at night. Keep room conversations short and quiet when somebody is resting, even if your own evening is only starting.

Pack what you will need before other guests settle down: clothes, toiletries, keys and charging cables. A little preparation avoids repeated trips in and out of lockers while the rest of the room is trying to sleep.

Use headphones thoughtfully

Do not play music, videos or voice messages through a speaker in a shared bedroom. Keep personal audio quiet enough that it does not become everyone else's soundtrack. For calls or a long video chat, use a suitable common area rather than the bunk beside a sleeping guest.

Take conversations elsewhere

Meet friends in a designated social space, not in the doorway of a dorm. Avoid letting doors slam, repeatedly rattling a locker or turning packing into a group conversation. If you have an early departure, organise your bag the evening before and finish noisy tasks outside the room where permitted.

Remember that corridors and stairwells may carry sound into other rooms. A common area is not automatically open all night; follow the hostel's arrangements and respect neighbours when leaving or returning.

2. Keep late-night light considerate

Give other guests a chance to sleep without bright light across their beds. Check which bedside or reading lights are available when you arrive, and keep anything needed for a late return within easy reach.

Use a small personal light

If you need a light, aim it at your own belongings or the floor rather than another person's face. Use the lowest practical brightness and avoid searching the whole room with a phone torch. Do not unplug somebody else's device or move their belongings to reach a socket.

Leave the main light alone

Do not switch on the room's main light for a late-night chat or to repack a suitcase. Prepare in an appropriate lit space where one is available. Ask staff where to go if the room setup does not give you a considerate option.

If you know you will need to work late, compare the actual common-space arrangements or a private-room option before booking. A room description is more useful than assuming every hostel has a separate overnight workspace.

3. Leave shared facilities ready for others

Use a kitchen, bathroom or lounge as if someone else will need it next. Wash up after cooking, wipe the surface you used and follow the property's food-storage and recycling instructions. Do not leave a sink full of dishes for another guest to deal with.

  • Keep shower and washbasin areas clear of your bags and toiletries after use.
  • Put food in the permitted storage area and label it if the hostel asks you to.
  • Allow others to use shared appliances and sockets rather than reserving them with belongings.
  • Tell staff about a spill, blocked drain or broken fitting instead of leaving it for the next person.

4. Keep belongings in your own space

A dorm bed does not include the neighbouring bunk or the route to the door. Keep shoes, bags and charging leads out of walkways. Do not hang items from another guest's bed, borrow their towel or move their luggage without asking.

Check the actual storage arrangement before you travel. A locker may have size limits or need your own lock, and bag storage before check-in or after checkout may have separate rules. Ask rather than assuming a large suitcase will fit or that storage is always staffed.

Keep travel documents, keys and valuables under your own control. If something goes missing, speak with staff and follow their process; do not search another person's belongings or film an accusation for the group chat.

5. Plan sleep arrangements without blame

People have different sleep patterns and needs. Discuss room preferences within your travel group before booking, especially if someone expects an early start or needs a quieter environment. A shared dorm is a compromise, not a promise of uninterrupted sleep.

If another guest is disturbing your rest, have a considerate conversation when they are awake or ask staff about available options. Do not record a sleeping person, wake them for a joke or turn a sleep issue into a public complaint about their body.

Choose the room, not a promised cure

A smaller dorm still means sharing. A private room may suit your preferences better, but availability, occupancy and price need checking with the property. Room choice is a practical accommodation decision; it is not a treatment for snoring.

For concerns about snoring, seek personal advice from a qualified clinician rather than selecting devices from a travel article. The NHS snoring information explains why treatment depends on the cause. No medical device or treatment is recommended here.

6. Socialise without taking over

Hostel common areas can be a good place to meet travellers, but not everyone wants the same evening. Invite people to join without repeatedly asking after they decline. A guest reading, working or resting does not need to explain why they are not joining the party.

Make activities optional

Let people choose what they drink and which games or activities they join. Do not make a purchase, a drinking challenge or a dare the price of being included. Agree on a budget before suggesting an event so friends can make an informed choice.

Plan the return before going out

Know how to get back, how the entrance works and what to do if reception is closed. Keep the address and access instructions available to each person in your group. Do not publish door codes or invite an unregistered visitor to use them.

Once back, move the conversation out of the dorm and leave other guests' beds alone. If a friend needs assistance, ask staff where available rather than making a sleeping stranger responsible for the situation.

7. Respect privacy and visitor rules

Ask before photographing people, including anyone who happens to be behind your selfie. Do not film sleeping guests or share another person's room number, belongings or private conversation. Agreement to pose for a photo is not automatic permission to post it.

Check the visitor and occupancy policy before bringing someone back. Keep intimate activity out of shared rooms and bathrooms. A booked bunk is not permission to add another person to the room, and other guests should not have to negotiate their privacy late at night.

Hostel etiquette questions

What is hostel etiquette?

It means using shared accommodation considerately: follow the property's rules, protect other people's sleep and privacy, keep your belongings contained and leave facilities ready for the next guest. Ask staff about rules that are unclear.

Can couples stay together?

Check the exact room and occupancy you are booking. A private room or a specifically listed shared-room arrangement may suit a couple, but do not assume two people can use a single booked bunk. Confirm the property's policy before paying.

What if I snore?

Choose accommodation with your own needs and the shared setting in mind, and discuss available room options with the property. Do not expect a smaller dorm to solve a sleep problem. Personal health concerns belong with a qualified clinician.

Will the bathroom be shared?

Read the listing: facilities may serve a room, a floor or a private booking. Check any access needs before travel. Wherever the bathroom is, leave it clean and avoid using it as a place to store belongings.

Make the shared stay work

Prepare before heading out, give others space to rest and ask about the property's actual arrangements. You can enjoy Krakow nightlife and still return as a considerate roommate. A little planning helps the whole group start the next day on better terms.
