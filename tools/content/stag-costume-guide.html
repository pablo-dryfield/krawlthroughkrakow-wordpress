A good stag do costume is one the person wearing it wants to wear, can move in and can change out of. Start with the groom's preferences, the group's budget and the plans for the evening, not a dare that someone has to endure.

For a Krakow night out, keep a normal outfit available and ask about the venues' entry requirements before choosing fancy dress. Read the KTK costume and dress guidance; a theme is not a promise of admission.

Choose a theme together

A shared colour, matching shirt or small accessory can give the group a theme without requiring everyone to buy a full costume. Agree a spending limit and make a plain-clothes option part of the plan.

Group themes without matching everything

Try retro sports shirts, a favourite film or a simple colour theme. Let people adapt it to clothes they already own, their comfort and how much luggage they can bring.

Agree the boundaries before the trip

Ask before buying an outfit for someone else. A guest should be able to say no, change their mind or skip a photograph without being pressured. Keep strangers and venue staff out of the joke.

Let the groom choose

The groom can stand out without being embarrassed. Offer a few choices and agree what happens if the outfit becomes uncomfortable or no longer suits the evening.

Wedding outfits and personal style

A wedding dress, suit or dramatic accessory can be a playful choice when the wearer enjoys it. Clothing and gender expression are not the punchline; do not dress someone up to ridicule an identity.

Costume ideas to compare

Choose around the actual itinerary. An outfit that works for a short daytime photo may be awkward for walking, dinner or a crowded venue later. A removable accessory is easier to adapt than an elaborate full-body costume.

Full-body suits: check the practicalities

Before packing a full-body suit, check that you can see clearly, move comfortably and remove the face covering. Think through pockets, a normal outer layer and changing clothes; do not assume a mask will be accepted at the door.

Superhero-inspired outfits

A themed shirt or colour combination can do the job without a bulky cape, rigid mask or prop. Check the plans with the group first rather than buying the most complicated costume you can find.

Be creative without making someone the joke

Choose a character, colour or fictional theme that people enjoy. Avoid outfits or slogans that target someone else's race, religion, sexuality, disability or appearance. An inside joke should be understood and welcomed by the person it concerns.

Makeup and drag-inspired looks

If the wearer wants a makeup or drag-inspired look, let them choose it. Leave time to get ready and change afterwards, and ask before sharing photos. It is a personal style choice, not a punishment for the groom.

Accessories can be easier to remove than a complete outfit. Check their size and practicality before adding them to the group luggage.

Animal costumes and onesies

A onesie can be a simple shared theme, but try it with the clothes and shoes you will actually wear. Consider walking, changing weather, bulky tails or hoods and where you will put the costume if you change for the evening.

Custom shirts: a simple group option

Matching shirts can be packed and changed easily. Confirm sizes, the design, total cost and delivery date before ordering. A shared colour also works if the group does not want to pay for printing.

Personalisation with boundaries

Check nicknames and slogans with the people wearing them. Avoid printing private information, unwanted photographs or a joke someone has already asked you not to use. Choose a design the whole group is happy to wear in public.

A practical DIY costume checklist

Build around ordinary clothes and an accessory you can remove. Try the complete outfit before travelling rather than discovering a problem when the group is ready to leave.

Put comfort first

Use shoes you are comfortable walking in and check the outfit while sitting, walking and using stairs. Keep your phone, booking details and ID easy to reach. Allow space for an ordinary layer if the weather changes.

Try it before the trip

  • Check that you can see, move and change clothes without help.
  • Pack a normal alternative rather than relying on buying one during the night.
  • Agree what to do with bulky accessories if the group changes plans.

Check the night's entry requirements

Ask the KTK team about fancy dress before joining and follow the venue's instructions on the night. The pub crawl FAQ recommends having an alternative outfit available. Do not treat a ticket, a theme or another group's previous visit as approval for your costume.

Ask before bringing masks or props

Explain what you plan to wear, including face coverings and large accessories. If the answer is unclear, leave the prop behind and choose a simpler version. A costume should not depend on staff making an exception.

Costume planning questions

What should I wear to a stag do?

Wear something you are comfortable walking and spending the evening in. A coordinated shirt or removable accessory is an option; check the particular venues and activities before choosing more elaborate fancy dress.

How do we choose a group theme?

Ask the groom and guests for preferences, set a budget and offer an easy alternative. The aim is to enjoy the celebration together, not to make someone wear an outfit they dislike.

Do we need fancy dress at all?

No theme is needed for a good group evening. If you prefer ordinary clothes, spend the planning time on an activity everyone wants, the meeting arrangements and a clear way to keep in touch.

Plan the outfit and the evening

Keep the costume optional, practical and easy to change. For the wider weekend, use the Krakow stag-group planning page to send your dates and activity shortlist. For the regular evening crawl, check how to join and the current meeting instructions.
