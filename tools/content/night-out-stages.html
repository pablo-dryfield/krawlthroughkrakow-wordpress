Why a numbered ladder is not useful

Alcohol's effects and the risk of overdose vary between individuals. A person can be at risk without acting out a familiar party stereotype. Alcohol can continue entering the bloodstream after someone stops drinking. NIAAA warns against assuming an unconscious person will be fine if left to sleep.

Read the official alcohol-overdose information rather than interpreting this title as clinical guidance. It is a US source, so use Poland's emergency number below, not the US number shown there. No drink count or target level is recommended here.

Leave drinking pressure out

Decide what you want from the night before somebody turns it into a competition. You can enjoy music, meet fellow travellers or leave early without accepting every round. A non-alcoholic order does not need an explanation, and a friend taking a break does not need encouragement to catch up.

Do not assign people roles such as the lightweight, the responsible one or the person who must finish the leftovers. An included offer or a game is not an obligation. Keep the group plan flexible enough for different choices rather than treating matching drinks as the point of going out.

Take a concern seriously

If someone seems unwell, stop treating the situation as part of the entertainment. Tell staff or a trusted person clearly what concerns you. Do not keep giving them drinks, challenge them to prove they are fine or leave because the rest of the group is moving on.

A photo, a joke or a reassuring message in the group chat is not an assessment. Stay focused on the person and on getting appropriate help. If you suspect an emergency, call rather than waiting for somebody else to confirm a label.

When urgent help is needed

Call 112 in Poland if you suspect alcohol poisoning, or someone cannot be woken, has a seizure or is breathing slowly, irregularly or not at all. Stay with them and follow the dispatcher's instructions. Do not wait for every warning sign or assume a person who has passed out should simply be put to bed.

The NHS urgent-help guidance is the medical source for this signposting. The Polish emergency-number page confirms the local number. Give a clear location and describe the concern. Do not delay calling to read more online.

Look after people without humiliating them

Do not film someone who is impaired, share private images or turn their condition into a public story. Move the attention away from jokes and towards getting help. Be clear about what you know and do not know when speaking with staff or responders.

Coffee, cold showers and walking are not an overdose treatment. Do not try a home remedy in place of urgent assistance. Keep the evening's plans secondary to the concern in front of you.

Respect boundaries throughout the night. Impairment is not an invitation for sexual attention, a prank or pressure to agree to something. The nightlife dating guide discusses respectful social choices without treating another person as a prize.

Plan the evening around your choices

Keep your own route home, accommodation details and a way to contact someone you trust. Let friends know when your plans change, and accept it when theirs change too. You do not have to complete a pub crawl or stay out until morning to enjoy Krakow.
